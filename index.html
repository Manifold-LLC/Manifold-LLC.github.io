01  Capabilities

What we do

Four kinds of engagement. Each one is scoped in writing before it starts and ends with a deliverable your office keeps.

1 / 4

Strategy and roadmaps

Where foundation models fit in a sponsor's portfolio, and where they do not yet. We write the analysis a program manager has to defend in front of a review board: what the technology can do today, what it will cost to run, and which parts should be bought rather than built.

Typical deliverables

  • Foundation-model strategy and capability roadmaps
  • Build-versus-buy analysis and technology forecasts
  • Technical SME on competitive acquisitions and BAA evaluation panels
  • AI/ML training curricula for sponsor staff
2 / 4

Acquisition and compute

Requirements that vendors can bid against, and compute plans that hold up to the budget. We draft technical requirements, RFP and SOW language, and source-selection criteria. We size GPU and TPU clusters against actual training and inference loads, in AWS, Azure, and Google GovCloud regions or on hardware you own.

Typical deliverables

  • Technical requirements and statement-of-work language
  • Source-selection and vendor-evaluation support
  • Cluster sizing and cost models for training and inference
  • GovCloud versus on-premises trade studies
3 / 4

Program assessment

An outside technical read on an AI program. Where it stands, what it will take to finish, and whether the metrics being briefed mean what they appear to mean. We have sat on the government side of these reviews and write them for the people who have to act on them.

Typical deliverables

  • Independent technical evaluation
  • Maturity and risk assessment
  • Milestone and design reviews
  • Sponsor-facing performance metrics
4 / 4

Secure generative AI

Getting a language-model system onto a controlled network and keeping it there. We design for Controlled Unclassified Information at Impact Level 4 and 5, build retrieval-augmented generation on GovCloud services, choose models and vendors with the accreditation path in mind, and do the evaluation work: bias audits and human-review design included.

Typical deliverables

  • Reference architectures for IL4 and IL5 environments
  • Retrieval-augmented generation design and delivery
  • Model and vendor selection in GovCloud
  • Evaluation plans, bias audits, and human-in-the-loop review design

03  Approach

How we work

We are a small firm and we run engagements like one. These are the rules we hold ourselves to.

  1. The people you meet do the work.

    A founding principal scopes the engagement, does it, and briefs it. Manifold does not keep a bench, and we do not hand work to junior staff after the kickoff.

  2. Written scope, fixed deliverable.

    Every engagement starts with a one-page scope and ends with something your office keeps: a report, a requirements package, or a working system. We would rather do a short, well-defined piece of work than hold an open-ended support seat.

  3. Sometimes the answer is not to build it.

    A fair share of our advice is that a problem is better handled with a rules engine, a database query, or a hire. We say so early. It is cheaper for you and better for the program.

  4. Current with the research, and with the hardware.

    We read the papers and run the models ourselves. When a vendor says its model does something, we can check, and we can tell you what it will cost per month to keep doing it.

  5. Cleared, and used to the environment.

    Our principals hold TS/SCI clearances and have worked in Intelligence Community spaces for years. Conversations can go where the problem needs them to go.

  6. Unclassified by default.

    This site, email, and first conversations stay unclassified. When a problem needs more than that, we move it to the right space with you.

04  Company

About Manifold

Manifold LLC is a Virginia small business formed by senior practitioners from the Intelligence Community.

We started the firm for a simple reason. The people deciding how to spend large sums on AI in government rarely have an advisor who has both run an acquisition and trained a model. We have done both. We are small on purpose and intend to stay that way.

The name comes from geometry. A manifold is a space that looks flat up close and is curved overall, which is a fair description of most AI programs we are asked to look at.

Working with us

Program offices can engage us directly as a small business. Integrators and primes bring us in as a subcontractor when a proposal or a program needs cleared AI/ML depth that is not on their staff. Either way, send a note and a principal will reply.
