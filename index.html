01  Capabilities

What we do

Five kinds of engagement. Each is scoped in writing before it starts, and each ends with a deliverable your office keeps. Four are advice. The fifth is building, and so is the delivery half of the fourth. We never advise and build for the same organization.

1 / 5

Strategy and roadmaps

Where foundation models fit in a sponsor's portfolio, and where they do not yet. We write the analysis a program manager has to defend in front of a review board: what the technology can do today, what it will cost to run, and which parts should be bought rather than built.

Typical deliverables

  • Foundation-model strategy and capability roadmaps
  • Build-versus-buy analysis and technology forecasts
  • Technical SME on competitive acquisitions and BAA evaluation panels
  • AI/ML training curricula for sponsor staff
2 / 5

Acquisition and compute

Requirements that vendors can bid against, and compute plans that hold up to the budget. We draft technical requirements, RFP and SOW language, and source-selection criteria. We size GPU and TPU clusters against actual training and inference loads in AWS GovCloud, Azure Government, and Google Cloud's assured regions, or on hardware you own.

Typical deliverables

  • Technical requirements and statement-of-work language
  • Source-selection and vendor-evaluation support
  • Cluster sizing and cost models for training and inference
  • Government cloud versus on-premises trade studies
3 / 5

Program assessment

An outside technical read on an AI program. Where it stands, what it will take to finish, and whether the metrics being briefed mean what they appear to mean. We have sat in these reviews before and write them for the people who have to act on them.

Typical deliverables

  • Independent technical evaluation, with maturity and risk assessment
  • Milestone and design reviews
  • Sponsor-facing performance metrics
4 / 5

Secure generative AI

Getting a language-model system onto a controlled network and keeping it there. We design for Controlled Unclassified Information at Impact Level 4 and 5, build retrieval-augmented generation in government cloud regions, choose models and vendors with the accreditation path in mind, and do the evaluation work, bias audits and human-review design included.

Typical deliverables

  • Reference architectures for IL4 and IL5 environments
  • Retrieval-augmented generation design and delivery
  • Model and vendor selection in government cloud regions
  • Evaluation plans and bias audits
  • Human-in-the-loop review design
5 / 5

Applied research and development

When a program needs something built rather than bought, we build it: detectors and trackers over satellite imagery, foundation models pretrained on a sponsor's own data, and the GPU or TPU kernels that make them affordable to run. We write the evaluation protocol before the model, score against data the model never sees, and report the negative results with the positive ones. Examples are under Selected work below.

Typical deliverables

  • Working prototypes with their evaluation harness and held-out test protocol
  • Pretrained domain models with scaling studies that say how big the next one should be
  • CPU, GPU and TPU implementations with measured cost per run on cloud hardware
  • Open-source releases where the sponsor allows them

  1. Research · Maritime domain awareness

    Sentinel-1 · Sentinel-2 · Landsat
    NISAR · VIIRS
    Multi-target tracking
    Unpublished draft

    Re-finding ships that switch off AIS, from one satellite pass to the next

    Problem
    Vessels involved in illegal fishing, sanctions evasion and smuggling switch off AIS, the transponder that reports a ship's identity and position. Satellites still see them, but passes are hours apart, and by the next one a moving ship can be any of many unexplained detections.
    What was built
    A tracker that links detections from public radar, optical and night-light sensors (Sentinel-1 and NISAR, Sentinel-2 and Landsat, VIIRS) across the waters around Denmark and southern Norway. Each sensor has its own detector and attribute model, including heading and speed read from Sentinel-2's band delay and wake, and the motion model is learned from historical AIS. The system was scored against full-rate Danish and Norwegian AIS that it never saw.
    Result
    Ships at anchor or in port were re-found in most cases. Ships under way were re-found far less often, and the true next sighting was usually within a short ranked list of candidates. No published system was found that measures this problem, so the results would be a first public baseline once published. Changes to the tracking algorithm did not improve the moving-ship result; adding sensors did, which points to more looks per day as the lever. Results are from an unpublished draft.

  2. Research · Synthetic aperture radar

    CPU · NVIDIA GPU · Google TPU
    C++ · CUDA · JAX Pallas
    Open source, MIT license
    Preprint, submitted to IEEE TGRS

    FastSAR: radar image formation on CPUs, GPUs and TPUs, compared on matched hardware

    Problem
    Every radar satellite returns raw phase histories that must be formed into images, and the fleet is growing fast. The open-source image-formation codes had never been compared on the same hardware against a common reference, and none ran on TPUs.
    What was built
    A library that forms SAR images on CPUs, NVIDIA GPUs and Google TPUs through one Python interface: exact backprojection, factorized backprojection and polar format, in several floating-point precisions, reading CPHD, SICD, Sentinel-1 and NISAR data. Every configuration was scored against a high-precision reference and priced at on-demand cloud rates, so a user can pick the accuracy, speed and cost they need.
    Result
    Faster than every open-source implementation tested, by orders of magnitude in most cases, at equal or better accuracy. A full commercial spotlight image forms in seconds on the lowest-priced cloud GPU for a fraction of a cent, and the comparison shows which precision and device to choose for interferometric, radiometric or quick-look use. Written with a co-author at Penn State's Applied Research Laboratory. The code is public under the MIT license on GitHub and installs from PyPI; the paper is on arXiv and submitted to IEEE Transactions on Geoscience and Remote Sensing.

  3. Research · Vessel movement

    Foundation model · AIS
    Under review

    A foundation model for where vessels go

    Problem
    Forecasting where a vessel will be, and what it is doing, underlies collision avoidance, search and rescue, port planning and fisheries enforcement. Each of those tasks has had its own small model.
    What was built
    A single model pretrained on years of public AIS traffic that forecasts a vessel's position as a probability map at any horizon, with small task heads on the frozen model for the questions analysts ask of a track.
    Status
    A paper is under review. We can discuss the approach, not the results, until it is published.

03  Approach

How we work

We are a small firm and we run engagements like one. These are the rules we hold ourselves to.

  1. The people you meet do the work.

    The practitioner who scopes an engagement does the work and briefs it. Nothing is handed to junior staff after the kickoff, and nobody is proposed to you who will not be in the room.

  2. A written scope and a fixed deliverable.

    Every engagement starts with a one-page scope and ends with something your office keeps, whether that is a report, a requirements package, or a working system. Typical engagements run from a few weeks to a few months. We take on only what we can deliver on the schedule we quote, and we say so when we cannot.

  3. Sometimes the answer is not to build it.

    A fair share of our advice is that a problem is better handled with a rules engine, a database query, or a hire. When that is the case we say so early, because it is cheaper for you and better for the program.

  4. Current with the research, and with the hardware.

    We read the papers and run the models ourselves, so when a vendor says its model does something we can check. We can also tell you what it will cost each month to keep doing it.

  5. Cleared.

    Everyone at Manifold holds a TS/SCI clearance with counterintelligence polygraph and has worked in Intelligence Community spaces for years.

  6. No conflicts of interest.

    We advise or we build, never both for the same organization. Where we help write requirements or evaluate bids, we do not compete for the resulting work, and we take no referral or reseller fees from any vendor. We do not pursue work at an organization where a member of the firm serves as a trusted agent or evaluator. That is what the word independent means on this page.

About Manifold

Manifold LLC is a Virginia small business founded by two practitioners who have spent their careers inside Intelligence Community research and acquisition.

We started the firm for a simple reason. The people deciding how to spend large sums on AI in government rarely have an advisor who has both worked an acquisition and trained a model. We have done both, and that pairing is the standard for everyone who works here.
