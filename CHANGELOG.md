### Added (photos)

- The hero drawing is now a live model: `assets/js/manifold.js` draws the same surface on a canvas, turns with a drag or the arrow keys, and carries a four-step lesson on the data manifold (data on a surface, locally flat, globally curved, off the surface). The opening step is "Why the name Manifold?". A hint above the drawing says it can be grabbed and turned, and dims once the visitor has turned it. Each step moves the camera with a smooth turn and zoom, the controls are a previous/next stepper that also answers the left and right arrow keys, and the text beneath cross-fades between steps. On the far-away step the true point and the model's point carry halos and backed labels, and the sample dots dim, so the gap between them reads at a glance. The last step shows an input inside the data but off the surface, lifted above the sampled cluster, for drift and sensor failures. The samples cover only part of the surface, and the extrapolation step ("Beyond the data") sends the flat guess into the unsampled part, so the error appears where the model has no data. A fifth step, "Why this matters", closes the lesson with the whole picture at once (a dashed outline where the data ends, the guess beyond it, the input off the surface), says Manifold does this as independent advisors or as the research and development team, and links to the contact form. Step 1 opens "Real data lives on a manifold" and says modern AI is, in large part, the attempt to learn that manifold from samples. The still SVG remains for visitors without JavaScript.
